How to Rank on Google Search: A Guide for Small Businesses

By Duke Chadha · SEO & Performance Marketing Consultant · Delhi NCR

Most small business owners try SEO themselves before they ever think about hiring someone — and most give up within a month because nothing seems to move. That's not because SEO doesn't work. It's because ranking on Google isn't one trick, it's five or six smaller things done consistently, in the right order. This is that order.

In this guide
  1. Get the foundation right first
  2. Claim and optimise your Google Business Profile
  3. Fix your on-page basics
  4. Publish content people are actually searching for
  5. Don't ignore technical health
  6. Earn a few real backlinks
  7. How long this actually takes

1. Get the foundation right first

Before writing a single blog post, make sure Google can actually find and understand your site. Two free tools do this:

  • Google Search Console — verify your site, submit a sitemap, and watch which queries you're already getting impressions for
  • Google Analytics — so you can tell traffic from ranking, not just guess

Skipping this step is the single most common mistake I see. You can't fix what you can't measure, and you can't measure what Google can't see in the first place.

2. Claim and optimise your Google Business Profile

If you serve customers locally — a clinic, a bakery, a service business, a consultancy — your Google Business Profile (GBP) often matters more than your website for the searches that actually bring in customers. Category, hours, photos, and consistent NAP (Name, Address, Phone) across the web all feed directly into whether you show up in the local map pack.

On a recent GBP engagement, focused optimisation work drove measurable local pack visibility gains for a Delhi NCR education business within weeks — faster than most on-site SEO wins, because GBP has less competition than the open web.

3. Fix your on-page basics

This is the unglamorous part, but it's foundational:

  1. Title tags — your main keyword close to the front, under 60 characters
  2. Meta descriptions — written to earn the click, not just describe the page
  3. One clear H1 per page — matching what the page is actually about
  4. Internal links — connect related pages so Google (and visitors) can find your best content

None of this is exciting. All of it compounds.

4. Publish content people are actually searching for

The biggest wasted effort I see is business owners writing about what they want to say, rather than what people are typing into Google. Before writing anything, check actual search volume and difficulty for your target keywords — a keyword research tool (I use Ubersuggest) will tell you in seconds whether a topic is worth pursuing or whether you're about to spend a week writing something nobody searches for.

"Rank for what people search, not for what you wish they searched."

One page, one primary keyword. Trying to rank a single page for five unrelated terms usually means it ranks for none of them well.

5. Don't ignore technical health

Even great content won't rank if the site itself is slow or broken. The three things worth checking regularly:

  • Core Web Vitals — particularly page load speed and layout stability on mobile, where most traffic now happens
  • Broken links and redirect chains — especially after any site migration or redesign
  • Mobile usability — Google indexes the mobile version of your site first, not desktop

I've seen sites lose ranking purely from a slow migration that left dozens of broken redirects behind — content and keywords were fine, but the technical debt was quietly capping everything.

6. Earn a few real backlinks

You don't need hundreds of backlinks. You need a handful of relevant, real ones — a mention from an industry directory, a partner site, a local business association, or a client linking back to you. Quality here beats quantity by a wide margin; a single relevant backlink often does more than fifty spammy directory links, and a spammy backlink profile can actively work against you.

7. How long this actually takes

4–8 weeksTo see initial movement on low-competition, long-tail keywords
3–6 monthsFor meaningful ranking gains on moderately competitive terms
OngoingSEO compounds — it's maintenance, not a one-time project

If someone promises page-one rankings in two weeks, that's the moment to be skeptical, not excited. Sustainable SEO is closer to compound interest than a light switch.
